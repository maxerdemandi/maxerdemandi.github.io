Max Erdemandi, PhD

Applied Statistics · Quantitative Methodology · Media & Social Psychology

I am a quantitative social scientist and methodologist. I study how bias and systematic error introduced by research design, measurement, and analytic choices shape the substantive conclusions drawn from data on human attitudes and behavior. I specialize in measurement and latent variable analysis, including structural equation and mixture models; Monte Carlo simulation and agent-based modeling; and machine learning and computational methods for network and language analysis. I also use scientometrics and curricular data to study how scientific knowledge is produced and disseminated across disciplinary boundaries.

I ground this methodological work in the study of information disorders and extreme beliefs, such as mis/disinformation, conspiracy theories, and violent extremism, across mass media, social identity, and risk and crisis contexts. I am an instructor and principal researcher in the Global Terrorism Studies program at the University of Maryland, and the current Vice Chair-Elect of the National Communication Association's Applied Communication Division. My work has appeared in Communication Quarterly, the Journal of Applied Communication Research, the NIJ Journal, and The Handbook of Fake News, Misinformation, and Disinformation.

Outside the academy, I evaluate large language models as an AI/LLM researcher. I also founded Rubrique Advisory Co., which helps colleges, universities, and mission-driven organizations measure and communicate the impact of their programs.

Research

My research asks how measurement and design choices shape what survey and text data appear to show, and applies that question to information disorders and extreme beliefs.

Quantitative methodology, psychometrics, and survey research

Multivariate, structural equation, mixture/latent class modeling that detect and correct the biases and systematic error that research design, measurement, and analytic choices introduce into substantive conclusions about human attitudes, beliefs, and behavior.

Computational analysis and machine learning

Machine learning, psycholinguistic, and computational methods for social network and text analysis, used to study how mis/disinformation, conspiracy theories, and extremist content spread and what their language reveals. Monte Carlo simulation and agent-based modeling test research design, measurement, and analytic choices before they reach real data.

Scientometrics and meta-science

Bibliometric and curricular data analyses to study how scientific knowledge is produced, taught, and disseminated in communication science and across the social sciences.

Information disorders and extreme beliefs

Conspiracy belief, mis/disinformation, and violent extremism across mass communication and media, social identity/intergroup, and crisis contexts.
